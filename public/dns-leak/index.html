Help

What is a DNS leak?

Before your browser connects to a site, a DNS resolver turns the name into an address. If that lookup skips the VPN or proxy, it goes to your own ISP's resolver: the ISP sees every site you visit, and sites that compare your resolver with your IP can tell you're using a VPN and where you actually are.

How does this test find my resolver?

The page looks up a random name under edns.ip-api.com that has never been asked before, so no cache can answer: your resolver has to ask ip-api's name server, which reports the resolver's address and network, and the client subnet if your resolver passes one along (ECS). The test compares the resolver's country with the country of your IP.

How do I fix a DNS leak?

Clash / Mihomo: enable its DNS with enhanced-mode fake-ip, or use TUN mode so every lookup goes through it. V2Ray / Xray clients: route DNS through the proxy (or set a DoH server that goes through it). In the browser, Secure DNS (DoH) also keeps lookups away from the ISP. Then run this test again.

My resolver is Cloudflare or Google in another city. Is that a leak?

Usually not. Public resolvers like 1.1.1.1 and 8.8.8.8 answer from the data center nearest to the request, so the city may differ from your VPN exit. What gives you away is a resolver run by your home ISP, or in your home country while your IP is abroad.
